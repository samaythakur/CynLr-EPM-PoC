# CynLr-EPM(Engineering Program Management)-PoC(Proof-of-Concept)

CynLr Engineering Program Management PoC
An Engineering Program Management case study developed around a robotics Proof of Concept involving visual tracking, robot coordination, and grasping of moving objects.
The project focuses on translating a technically complex robotics PoC into an executable program plan using Asana, with emphasis on phased execution, cross-functional coordination, dependencies, external risks, resource planning, KPIs, testing, and final delivery.

PoC means Proof of Concept - It means you were given a robotics use case/scenario and your job was to create a structured program plan for how that technical PoC could be executed. You did not build CynLr's actual robot or claim that the system was physically implemented.

Key Objectives:-
• Structure the robotics PoC into executable phases and workstreams
• Define task, intra-project, and external dependencies
• Coordinate robotics, computer vision/ML, software, mechanical, electronics, and external teams
• Identify risks, bottlenecks, and resource constraints
• Define KPIs, lead/lag indicators, and progress-balancing mechanisms
• Estimate skill hours and map resources to activities
• Establish testing, review, and final handover criteria

Some Questions-

What did you do in this project?
This was an Engineering Program Management PoC based on a CynLr robotics use case. My role was to take the technical objective and convert it into an executable program plan. I broke the work into two technical phases—moving-object tracking and track-and-grasp—and then structured the supporting hardware integration, testing, external dependencies, risks, resource planning and final delivery activities in Asana. I also mapped task dependencies, identified the different engineering disciplines involved, estimated skill hours in a resource-planning sheet, and created KPIs with lead and lag indicators to monitor both execution and technical outcomes.

What exactly did you personally build?
I built the program-management framework rather than the physical robotics system. I created the Asana work breakdown structure, linked dependencies between tasks, identified external dependencies and bottlenecks, mapped resources across robotics, vision/ML, software, mechanical and electronics, estimated engineering effort, and defined the KPI and progress-monitoring framework. I then documented the complete approach and supporting data in Excel/Google Sheets.

Why do you call it a PoC?
Because the assignment was focused on demonstrating how the proposed robotics objective could be planned and validated as a proof-of-concept program. My contribution was the Engineering Program Management layer—how the work would be organized, coordinated, measured and delivered.
